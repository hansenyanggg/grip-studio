    The three photos
    • Photo 1 (palm up): tape lying along the fingers, its zero at the crease where the hand meets the wrist, fingertips lightly against the tape edge. Hold the camera straight overhead. Gives hand length, finger lengths, thumb length.
    • Photo 2 (palm down): back of the hand up, tape lying across the hand, touching it. Gives hand breadth, thumb breadth, and finger widths.
    • Photo 3 (side view): hand resting on its side so ONE finger is seen from the side, with the tape upright behind that finger, camera straight-on. Gives ONE finger thickness; the other three are estimated.
    • All three: cm edge numbers only, no shadows across the creases, whole hand + tape in frame, camera level (use the level indicator).
    How the estimated thicknesses work
    • You tap ONE finger thickness in photo 3 and say which finger it was.
    • The other three are estimated from the finger widths you measured in photo 2, using a typical thickness-to-width ratio for each finger (index 1.00, middle 0.87, ring 0.95, little 0.83).
    • Thumb thickness is always estimated: about 0.88 × thumb breadth (from photo 2).
    • Estimated values are marked "est." in the table and flagged in the JSON under _estimated.
    • These ratios come from one carefully measured hand, so they are a reasonable starting point for any hand; expect a few mm of difference on other people. A wrong estimate only slightly changes the depth of that finger's groove.
    • Grip diameter is estimated as (thumb length + middle finger length + thumb thickness) ÷ π. It is a rough estimate; a grip diameter measured with a separate tool always takes precedence.
    • If the middle finger is missing, the longest finger you have stands in for it in that estimate. If the thumb is missing, no estimate is possible: type your measured grip diameter in the Generate section.
    • If a finger is present but you skip one of its taps, its length or width is filled with the average of your other measured values and marked "est.".
